INDUMIL: ballistic helmet technology transfer

I formulated and led the model that took Colombia’s state defence manufacturer from lacking the technology to negotiating its full transfer: design, manufacturing, assembly, testing and maintenance of level IIIA ballistic helmets.

#The starting point: a market without the technology

Colombia's state defence manufacturer wanted to enter the ballistic protection market, and its own tender bases said it plainly: it had neither the know-how nor the infrastructure, production lines or facilities to make level IIIA ballistic helmets.

Buying the technology turnkey was expensive and guaranteed no demand. Building it from scratch was slow. The challenge was to design a business in which selling first would pay for learning next.

#Designing the transfer as a business

The answer was a business collaboration agreement: a six-year framework agreement in which INDUMIL and a technology partner jointly sell ballistic helmets to the Armed Forces, the National Police and other state entities, with the partner obliged to transfer the technology.

The sequence was written into the bases:

  1. Joint commercialisation through purchase orders.
  2. Feasibility study (technical, legal, market, financial and IP valuation).
  3. Derived agreements for the transfer, with projected sales, capex and opex.
  4. Training, transfer, on-the-job training and technical assistance.

Key point

Taking the transfer is INDUMIL's right and the partner's obligation. The technology risk is decided with market data in hand, not before.

#Two rounds to lower the risk

Instead of a single tender, the process ran in two rounds:

  • First round (IPO 109/2017): pre-qualified bidders with at least five years of manufacturing and assembly experience and asked each for an RFI with its own commercialisation, transfer and intellectual property model.
  • Second round (IPO 220/2017): what the RFI taught became firm requirements. A 100% functional turnkey plant, transfer of 100% of the parts of three helmet models and their accessories, and evaluation on price, lower weight, lower trauma and an incentive for national industry.

#What INDUMIL receives

  • Intangibles: know-how in design, manufacturing, assembly, testing and maintenance, and a perpetual licence for the products in Colombia and the rest of the Americas, with the right to improve them.
  • Tangibles: the technological process in operation, with its machinery, equipment and instrumentation, and the technical documentation of product and process.
  • Capabilities: 440 hours of specialised training, on-the-job training and five years of technology updates.

#The outcome

The process ended in a manufacturing improvement and technology transfer contract with an Israeli company: USD 10M in gross income and USD 3M in intangibles for the state manufacturer.

#My role

I led the project from its formulation: the diagnosis, the design of the collaboration model and of the two rounds, the structuring of the transfer and the negotiation of the contract.
